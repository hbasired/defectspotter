Why not a defect classifier? It needs hundreds of labelled images per defect type — defects are ≈ 1% of parts — and a new defect type passes unseen. DefectSpotter learns “normal” instead.

For every part: an anomaly score, a heat map of where, and a decision

How to read it

Two families of detectors: two use the ResNet, the other two work on raw pixels

The same image goes to every detector; each path ends with its accuracy (mean image AUROC, 1.0 = perfect).

The ResNet is never trained. ImageNet weights (torchvision), used frozen as a feature extractor.
ImageNet is data, not a model — 1.28 M photos the weights were learned on.
ResNet → VAE only for ②. The pixel VAE ③ and the baseline ④ never see the ResNet.
⑤ Ensemble = w·① + (1−w)·② — ties with ① but runs two models, so ① is deployed.

Thresholds are chosen with painted defects — no real defect image is needed

Two synthetic defects on every held-out good part, replayed at 50% and 25% strength; 12 per product refined by Stable Diffusion.

Procedural: cut-paste · scratch · stain (exact masks)

Generative AI: Stable Diffusion 1.5 SDEdit + LCM-LoRA (6 steps, strength 0.45, ≈ 17 s per image on a CPU)

A defect set may influence the threshold only if the detector can see it (synthetic AUROC ≥ 0.9) — faint 25% replays mark the detection limit instead.

Models 4 & 5 — the classical baseline and the ensemble are references, not winners

Both are built and calibrated like the others; the comparison shows what they add.

④ Mean-image baseline (classical)

image → OpenCV ECC alignment → |pixel − mean| / max(std, 4) → blur → top-1% mean
  • alignment chosen on synthetic data: affine / affine / none
  • image AUROC: 0.975 / 0.277 / 0.435 (bottle / screw / carpet)
  • Verdict: fine on the fixed bottle view, fails on rotating screws and on texture → learned features are needed.

⑤ Ensemble (PatchCore + feature VAE)

score = w · PatchCore + (1 − w) · feature-space VAE (both on the 0–100 scale)
  • w chosen on held-out good + synthetic defects: 1.0 / 0.9 / 0.8
  • image AUROC: 1.000 / 0.994 / 0.938 — mean 0.978 vs PatchCore 0.979
  • Verdict: a statistical tie that runs two models (155 vs 88 ms per part) → not deployed.

One 0–100 scale and cost-optimal PASS / CHECK / REJECT thresholds — set without real defects

Same procedure for every product and detector; the labelled-defect threshold is computed only to check it.

score = 25 + 50 · (raw − median good) / spread
25 = typical held-out good part · 75 = typical synthetic defect
cost per part = (1−p)·FRR·2 + p·escapes·400 + checks·0.30
p = 1% defects · Gaussian-smoothed rates · CHECK ≤ 5% of parts
Grid: threshold 0–100 in steps of 0.5 × CHECK half-band 0–15 → the cheapest pair is deployed.

Camera and lighting drift

  • Health gate on every image: brightness, contrast, sharpness z-score; |z| > 4 → no automatic decision, part → CHECK.
  • EWMA control charts on scores and camera signals (λ = 0.2, ±3σ, 2 points in a row) + PSI of the score histogram.
  • Camera moved → fix the camera; good parts score higher → new lot → re-qualify.

Takeaways

1

Good parts are enough. No defect image trains a model or sets a threshold — painted and diffusion defects stand in.

2

Five detectors, one winner. PatchCore on a frozen ImageNet ResNet-18: mean AUROC 0.979, best on every product.

3

ResNet and VAE are separate paths. Only the feature-space VAE uses ResNet features; it beats the pixel VAE (0.899 vs 0.683).

4

Decisions priced in money. PASS / CHECK / REJECT from a cost model: 0% false rejects, ≤ 1.4 inspectors per shift, 71% cheaper than manual.

5

Safe in operation. Camera-health gate, drift charts, human review with audit log, label-free re-qualification for new lots.
